Dry Fitting: Use a Caliper to Measure Tolerances

When dry fitting parts, use a digital caliper to measure the tolerances between mating surfaces. This helps identify potential issues before assembly, allowing for adjustments to be made or parts to be replaced. Record the measurements to track progress and make informed decisions.

2

Structural Support: Use a Combination of Pins and Clamps

When building complex structures, use a combination of pins and clamps to provide temporary support. Pins can be used to hold parts in place, while clamps can be used to apply pressure and secure the structure. This approach helps prevent damage and ensures accurate alignment.

3

Modifying Parts: Use a Dremel with a Carbide Bit for Precise Cutting

When modifying parts, use a Dremel with a carbide bit for precise cutting. The carbide bit's high hardness and sharpness enable accurate cuts in a variety of materials, including plastic and metal. Apply a light touch and use a dust mask to minimize debris and ensure a smooth finish.

4

Seam Filling: Use a Cyanoacrylate-Based Adhesive for Strong Bonds

When filling seams, use a cyanoacrylate-based adhesive for strong bonds and minimal shrinkage. Apply a small amount to the seam, then use a brush or cloth to smooth it out. Allow the adhesive to cure before adding additional coats or finishing the surface.

5

Construction Sequencing: Build in a Logical Order to Minimize Errors

When building complex models, construct in a logical order to minimize errors and rework. Start with the largest components and work your way down to the smallest details. This approach helps prevent damage to smaller parts and ensures that each component is accurately fitted and aligned.
