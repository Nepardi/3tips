Thinning Vallejo Model Air for Airbrushing

Vallejo Model Air is already thin for airbrushing, but for fine nozzles (0.2mm), mix 1:1 with Tamiya Plastic Cement (T-300) to ensure even flow. Use 1.5-2.0 bar pressure. Test on a white plastic spoon — if it flows smoothly without spidering, it's ready. Add 1-2 drops of Alclad II Flow Aid (A-42) per 10 drops of mixed paint to prevent clogging in tight areas like cockpit details.

Base Coat with Vallejo Model Color

Apply Vallejo Model Color as a base coat using a 1:1 ratio with Vallejo Airbrush Thinner (71.261). Use a 1.5mm airbrush tip for even coverage. Spray 2-3 light coats, allowing each to dry completely. For metallics, use a 1.0mm tip and spray at 1.2 bar to avoid runs. Test on a white plastic spoon — if the paint forms a thin layer without sagging, it’s correctly thinned.

Camouflage Masking with Scale75 Masking Tape

Use Scale75 Masking Tape for precise camouflage patterns. Apply with a small brush using Tamiya Plastic Cement (T-300) to ensure adhesion. Cut patterns with a scalpel and apply to the model. For multi-color schemes, peel off the tape after each color layer to avoid muddying. Clean the tape with a damp cloth before reapplying to maintain sharp edges.

Applying Washes with AK Interactive and AMMO

Mix AK Interactive Wash (506) with AMMO by Mig Jimenez Dust (M-002) in a 1:1 ratio. Apply with a 1.5mm airbrush tip at 1.0 bar. For fine details, use a sponge brush with a 1:3 dilution. Test on a white plastic spoon — if it spreads evenly without pooling, it’s ready. For a dry brush effect, dab the sponge lightly over the surface to create texture.

Gloss Coat Before Decals with Clear Prop Models

Apply a thin gloss coat of Clear Prop Models Gloss Varnish (C-117) before decals. Spray 1-2 light coats, allowing 15 minutes to dry between each. Use a 1.5mm airbrush tip at 1.2 bar for even coverage. Test on a white plastic spoon — if it forms a smooth layer without orange peel, it’s correctly applied. This prevents decal lifting and ensures sharp, vibrant prints.
